The 35-Hour Gameplan

Today: Sat 20 Sept 2026. Exam: Sun 29 Nov 2026. Runway: 10 weeks.

Budget: 35 hours. Stated capacity: 3–5 h/wk for a month, then 5–7 h/wk. Those two numbers don't reconcile (your stated weekly rate over 10 weeks is ~52h), so 35 is the binding constraint and this plan is built to 35. There's an overflow list at the bottom for the extra hours if they materialise.

Allocation

Phase Weeks Hours Purpose
0 — Calibrate 1 4 Find out where you actually stand
1 — Build 2–4 11 Fix the weak side, speed up the strong side
2 — Mock cycle 5–8 14 Exam-craft. This is where the percentile is made.
3 — Peak & taper 9–10 6 One last rep, then get out of your own way
35

Roughly 55% of your hours go to mocks and mock analysis, not to learning content. That's deliberate and it's correct for your profile. You don't have a knowledge gap. You have an exam-craft gap, and exam-craft is only built under the clock.

PHASE 0 — Week 1 (Sun 20 – Sun 27 Sept) · 4 hours

Session 0.1 — Admin (30 min) · DO TODAY

  • Register at iimcat.ac.in. Deadline Mon 22 Sept, 5 PM.
  • Read 01-the-exam.md and 03-playbook.md end to end.

Session 0.2 — Cold diagnostic (2h) · This weekend, ideally Sunday morning

Take CAT 2024 Slot 2 as a full mock. Real conditions:

  • 2 hours, no breaks, no phone, no water break mid-section
  • Sectional timers enforced — set three 40-minute alarms and stop dead when each rings
  • Rough paper and a pen only. No calculator app.
  • Do it in the 8:30–10:30 AM window. You don't know your slot yet, but morning is the most likely and it's the hardest to be sharp for.

Do not prepare for this. The point is an uncontaminated reading.

Session 0.3 — Analysis (1.5h) · Same day, after a break

This is not "check the answer key." For every question you got wrong or skipped, tag it:

Tag Meaning Fix
KNOWLEDGE Didn't know the concept/method Learn it (rare for you, expect ~0 in QA)
SLOW Knew it, ran out of time Drill for speed
CARELESS Knew it, executed wrong Process discipline
SELECTION Shouldn't have opened this question at all Playbook discipline — the big one
BLIND Read it, had no idea where to start Pattern exposure

Then compute, per section: attempts, accuracy, raw score, estimated percentile (table in 01-the-exam.md).

Write the results into tracker.md. The rest of this plan reallocates around that number.

The reallocation rule

Default Phase 1 split is VARC 5h / DILR 4h / QA 2h. Adjust:

  • VARC diagnostic below 80th percentile → VARC 7h / DILR 3h / QA 1h. This is the emergency case and it's the most likely one.
  • VARC 80–90 → keep the default. You're on track for the floor.
  • VARC above 90 → VARC 3h / DILR 5h / QA 3h, and raise your overall target to 99.7.
  • QA below 95th percentile → something's wrong; it's arithmetic. Probably SLOW or CARELESS, not KNOWLEDGE. Add 1h of drills, take it from DILR.

PHASE 1 — Weeks 2–4 (28 Sept – 18 Oct) · 11 hours

Roughly 3.5–4 h/week. Split it into 2 sessions a week, 1.5–2h each. Don't do 20-minute sessions; the section is 40 minutes and you need to train that endurance.

VARC — 5 hours

The only thing that moves VARC in 5 hours is volume of timed passages plus ruthless wrong-answer autopsy. Not vocabulary. Not grammar. Not "reading more books."

Every session, same protocol:

  1. Pick 3 RC passages from real past CAT papers.
  2. 8 minutes per passage, hard stop, all 4 questions attempted. Timer visible.
  3. Then, for every wrong answer, write one sentence: why the right answer is right, and specifically which word or clause in the passage supports it. Then one sentence: what made the wrong option attractive.

That second sentence is the entire exercise. CAT wrong options fail in a small number of stereotyped ways, and once you can name them you stop picking them:

Trap What it looks like
Too strong Uses always, must, proves, impossible where the passage said tends to, suggests
Half-right First clause is textually supported, second clause is invented. You read the first clause and stopped.
Out of scope True, sensible, and not in the passage
Reversal States the view the author is arguing against as if it were the author's
Detail-as-main-idea Accurately describes paragraph 3, offered as the answer to "the primary purpose of the passage"

Target: 15–18 passages across Phase 1. Start with questions/varc-set-01.md for the worked example of this protocol.

Free background reps (these do not count against your 35 hours): read one long-form essay every other day from Aeon, Psyche, 3 Quarks Daily, The Browser, London Review of Books, or The Caravan. CAT passages are drawn from exactly this register. Twenty minutes of it while you eat is free percentile. You are not studying — you are just becoming a person for whom a 600-word argument about aesthetics isn't foreign terrain.

DILR — 4 hours

Protocol: 4 sets per session, 12 minutes each, hard stop. If you haven't cracked the set at 12:00, stop, and then look at the solution and ask only one question: what was the entry point I missed?

DILR sets have exactly one entry point — the constraint that collapses the possibility space first. Finding it in 90 seconds is the whole skill. Everything after it is bookkeeping you can already do.

Build a mental taxonomy as you go. By the end of Phase 1 you should be able to look at a set and name it in 10 seconds:

  • Arrangement grid (people × attributes × positions)
  • Tournament / round-robin with partial results
  • Scheduling with precedence constraints
  • Voting / elimination rounds with redistribution
  • Network or flow (routes, capacities, costs)
  • Partially-filled table with row/column totals plus a logical rider
  • Venn / set-overlap with min-max questions
  • Games with optimal play

Target: 16–20 sets. Start with questions/dilr-set-01.md.

QA — 2 hours

You do not need to learn maths. You need to not lose time. Two sessions:

Session A (1h) — arithmetic speed. Run questions/qa-arithmetic-drill.md, 20 questions in 25 minutes. Then spend the rest of the hour on whichever arithmetic sub-topic you were slowest in. Arithmetic is 45% of the section and the fastest-scoring part of the entire exam.

Session B (1h) — full sectional sim. questions/qa-set-01.md, 22 questions, 40 minutes, three-pass method from the playbook. The goal of this session is not score. It is to practise abandoning questions. Deliberately skip the three hardest-looking ones without guilt.

Things to explicitly re-memorise (10 minutes, once): squares to 30, cubes to 15, reciprocals as percentages (1/7 = 14.28%, 1/8 = 12.5%, 1/9 = 11.11%, 1/11 = 9.09%, 1/12 = 8.33%, 1/13 = 7.69%), Pythagorean triples, and the standard inradius/circumradius formulae. This is the only rote work in the plan.

PHASE 2 — Weeks 5–8 (19 Oct – 15 Nov) · 14 hours

One full mock per week, plus deep analysis. This is the core of the whole plan.

Per week: 2h mock + 1h15 analysis = 3h15. Four weeks = 13h. Plus 1h of targeted patching = 14h.

Mock protocol

  • Same time of day every week. Saturday or Sunday morning, 8:30 AM start.
  • Full 2 hours, sectional timers, no interruptions. Treat a phone glance as a failed rep.
  • Buy a proper mock series (see 04-resources.md) — the percentile benchmarking against a real cohort of ~20,000 is worth the ₹2–3k on its own. Your raw score means nothing without it.

Analysis protocol (1h15 — do it the same day, never skip it)

This is where the percentile actually comes from. A mock you don't analyse is 2 hours thrown away.

  1. (15 min) Re-tag every miss with the five-tag taxonomy from Phase 0. Add the counts to tracker.md.
  2. (20 min) Selection audit — the important one. Go back through the paper ignoring whether you got things right, and answer:
  3. Which questions did I open that I should have passed on? (Cost: time.)
  4. Which questions did I skip that I could have solved in under 90 seconds? (Cost: 3 marks each. This is usually the single biggest leak.)
  5. In DILR: did I read all four sets before starting? Did I abandon at 12 minutes?
  6. In QA: how many minutes did my longest question take? If the answer is over 3, that is your finding.
  7. (25 min) Re-solve. Every question you got wrong or skipped, solved properly now, on paper. Not read — solved.
  8. (15 min) Write three lines in tracker.md: what I'll do differently next mock. Three concrete behavioural rules, not "be more careful."

Week-by-week focus

Week Mock Analysis emphasis
5 Mock 1 Baseline under a real cohort. Find your natural attempt counts.
6 Mock 2 Selection discipline. Enforce the 12-min DILR abandon rule and the QA three-pass.
7 Mock 3 VARC floor. Deliberately attempt only 3 passages plus all 8 VA. Check whether accuracy rises enough to beat a 4-passage rush.
8 Mock 4 Full dress rehearsal. Whatever your best configuration turned out to be, execute it exactly.

The 1 hour of patching

Spend it on whatever your tag counts say. If SELECTION dominates, the fix is re-reading 03-playbook.md and doing a DILR set-selection drill: read 8 sets, rank them, solve only the top 3. If SLOW in QA dominates, more arithmetic drills. If BLIND in DILR dominates, more set-type exposure.

PHASE 3 — Weeks 9–10 (16 – 29 Nov) · 6 hours

Week 9 (16–22 Nov) · 4 hours

  • Sat 21 Nov: Mock 5, final full mock (2h) + analysis (1h). Run it at your actual allotted slot time — you will have the admit card by now (released around early Nov). This is the last mock. Do not take another.
  • (1h) Error-log sweep. Re-solve every question you've gotten wrong across all five mocks. You will find the same three or four failure modes repeating. Those are your real opponents.

Week 10 (23–29 Nov) · 2 hours — taper

No new material after 22 Nov. None. New material this late only produces anxiety.

  • (30 min) Logistics. Print the admit card. Confirm your ID (original photo ID matching the application, exactly). Actually travel to the test centre and look at it — know the gate, the parking, the travel time from your place at that hour on a Sunday.
  • (1h) Light touch. One DILR set and five QA questions every other day, 20 minutes. Purely to keep the machinery warm. No mocks, no timing pressure, no scoring.
  • (30 min) Re-read 03-playbook.md on Friday 27th. That is the last prep act.
  • Sat 28 Nov: nothing. Sleep. This is a real instruction, not a pleasantry — CAT is a sustained-attention test and sleep debt costs more than any 2 hours of revision gains.

Sat or Sun AM 1 × 90–120 min session Mock, 8:30 AM, 2h

Protect the weekend morning block above everything else. A mock taken at 11 PM on a Tuesday trains nothing useful.

If you have more than 35 hours

Your stated weekly rate suggests you might end up nearer 50. If so, add in this order — the list is ranked by marginal percentile per hour:

  1. +6h: two more mocks (weeks 6 and 8 get a second one). Mocks are still the highest-return hour you can spend, right up until the last week.
  2. +4h: 12 more RC passages. The VARC floor is the biggest single risk in your paper, and passage volume is the only lever that works on this timescale.
  3. +3h: DILR set-selection drills. Read 10 sets, rank them by tractability in 5 minutes, solve only the top 3. Repeat. This trains the exact 5 minutes that decides your DILR score.
  4. +2h: QA sectional sims from past papers, full 40 minutes each.

Do not spend extra hours on: advanced maths topics, vocabulary lists, grammar rules, or "completing the syllabus." None of them are on the critical path, and CAT has no syllabus worth completing.
