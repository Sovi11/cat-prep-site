The 30-second version of what CAT is

A 2-hour, 68-question aptitude test. Three sections, 40 minutes each, strictly sequential — you cannot go back:

# Section Qs Marks What it actually is
1 VARC 24 72 4 dense humanities passages + 8 verbal questions
2 DILR 22 66 4 logic/data puzzles, 5–6 questions each
3 QA 22 66 Class 8–10 maths, done fast

Scoring: +3 correct, −1 wrong on MCQs, 0 penalty on TITA (type-in-the-answer, ~15–20 of the 68). Max 204.

Percentile is computed after normalising across the three slots, so your score is relative to ~300,000 people, not to an absolute bar.

The thing nobody tells you: CAT is not a knowledge test. Everyone in the 99th percentile can solve every question given unlimited time. The entire exam is question selection under a time constraint. You are market-making with 22 instruments and 40 minutes.

Your read on this

You have an edge most candidates don't and a hole most candidates don't have.

  • QA is free money for you. It's arithmetic, ratios, basic algebra and mensuration. Your only risk is over-solving — spending 4 minutes on an elegant problem and losing two easy ones.
  • DILR should be your best section. It's pure constraint propagation. Your risk is falling in love with a set that won't crack.
  • VARC is where IIT/quant people die. Not because it's hard — because the "correct" answer feels arguable, and the passages are art criticism and post-colonial historiography, not things you read for pleasure. This is where 30% of your hours go.

Realistic target with 35 hours: 99.0–99.5 overall, with VARC as the variance. See 01-the-exam.md for the attempt maths.
