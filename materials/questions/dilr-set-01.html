DILR — Sectional Simulation #1

3 sets · 17 questions · 36 minutes

Real CAT gives you 4 sets in 40 minutes. This is a 3-set version at the same per-set pace.

Run it exactly like the real thing: 1. Spend the first 4 minutes reading all three sets and ranking them GO / MAYBE / NO. Solve nothing. Write your ranking down before you start — you'll want to check it against reality afterwards. 2. Then 12 minutes per set, hard stop. If a set isn't cracked at 12:00, abandon it and move on. Write your start time for each set on your rough sheet. 3. Build the complete solution before answering any question. Then answer all of them in 90 seconds.

Solutions at the bottom.

SET 1 — The Trading Floor (6 questions)

Five traders — Arjun, Bela, Chirag, Divya and Esha — sit at five desks numbered 1 to 5 from left to right. Each trader covers exactly one of five underlyings: NIFTY, BANKNIFTY, RELIANCE, HDFC and INFY. At the end of the day, each has a distinct P&L from the set {−2, +1, +3, +5, +8} (in ₹ lakh).

The following is known:

  1. Chirag sits at desk 3 and his P&L was +3.
  2. The trader covering INFY sits at desk 1.
  3. The trader at desk 5 had a P&L of +8.
  4. Divya was the only trader to lose money.
  5. Bela's P&L was higher than Arjun's, which was higher than Esha's.
  6. Divya sits at a desk adjacent to Chirag's.
  7. The NIFTY trader sits immediately to the left of the BANKNIFTY trader.
  8. The trader covering RELIANCE had a P&L of +1.
  9. Bela does not cover HDFC.

1.1 Who covers HDFC?   (a) Arjun    (b) Bela    (c) Chirag    (d) Esha

1.2 (TITA) What was the P&L, in ₹ lakh, of the trader at desk 2?

1.3 (TITA) At which desk does Divya sit?

1.4 The trader sitting immediately to the right of the RELIANCE trader covers:   (a) NIFTY    (b) BANKNIFTY    (c) HDFC    (d) INFY

1.5 (TITA) What is the combined P&L, in ₹ lakh, of the traders at desks 1 and 4?

1.6 (TITA) If condition 9 were removed and all other conditions retained, how many distinct complete arrangements would be possible?

SET 2 — Round Robin (5 questions)

Four teams — P, Q, R and S — play a round-robin tournament in which every team plays every other team exactly once. A win earns 3 points, a draw earns 1 point to each team, and a loss earns 0.

At the end of the tournament the points tally was: P = 7, Q = 4, R = 4, S = 1.

It is also known that R did not draw with S.

2.1 (TITA) How many of the six matches ended in a draw?

2.2 (TITA) How many matches did S lose?

2.3 Which team definitely lost to R?   (a) P    (b) Q    (c) S    (d) Cannot be determined

2.4 Which of the following is certainly true?   (a) Q drew with R    (b) P beat Q    (c) S drew with P    (d) R lost to Q

2.5 (TITA) In how many distinct ways could the six results have occurred?

SET 3 — Desk P&L (6 questions)

A firm reports quarterly P&L (in ₹ crore) for its four trading desks. Some figures have been withheld.

Desk Q1 Q2 Q3 Q4 Year
Equity 12 — 18 — 61
Commodity — 9 — 14 44
FX 8 — 11 7 38
Rates — 15 — 10 48
Total 40 47 53 51 191

It is additionally known that the Commodity desk's Q3 P&L was exactly twice its Q1 P&L.

3.1 (TITA) What was the Equity desk's Q4 P&L?

3.2 Which desk recorded the largest percentage increase from Q2 to Q3?   (a) Equity    (b) Commodity    (c) FX    (d) Rates

3.3 (TITA) In how many of the four quarters did the FX desk out-earn the Rates desk?

3.4 (TITA) What was the Rates desk's Q1 P&L?

3.5 The two highest-earning desks for the full year together accounted for approximately what share of the firm's annual P&L?   (a) 51%    (b) 54%    (c) 57%    (d) 62%

3.6 If the additional condition about the Commodity desk were dropped, which of these cells could no longer be determined uniquely?   (a) FX Q2    (b) Equity Q2    (c) Equity Q4    (d) Rates Q1

SOLUTIONS

SET 1 — The Trading Floor

The entry point is clue 4 plus clue 5. Divya is the only loss, so Divya = −2. Chirag = +3 (clue 1). That leaves {+1, +5, +8} for Arjun, Bela and Esha, and clue 5 orders them completely: Bela = +8, Arjun = +5, Esha = +1. All five P&Ls are now fixed before you've placed anyone. That's the collapse — everything after it is forced.

Then: - Desk 5 has +8 (clue 3) → Bela at desk 5. - RELIANCE trader has +1 (clue 8) → Esha covers RELIANCE. - Desk 1 is INFY (clue 2). Chirag is at 3, Bela at 5, and Esha covers RELIANCE not INFY. Divya must be at desk 2 or 4 (clue 6). So desk 1 is Arjun, covering INFY. - Divya and Esha take desks 2 and 4 in some order.

Two cases, resolved by clue 7 (NIFTY immediately left of BANKNIFTY) and clue 9:

  • Divya at 2, Esha at 4: RELIANCE sits at desk 4, and the only adjacent pair left among desks 2, 3, 5 is (2, 3). That forces HDFC to desk 5 — but that's Bela, violating clue 9. ✗
  • Divya at 4, Esha at 2: RELIANCE at desk 2. NIFTY/BANKNIFTY must be at (3, 4) or (4, 5). If (3, 4), HDFC lands on desk 5 = Bela. ✗ So it's (4, 5), with HDFC at desk 3. ✓

Final arrangement:

Desk 1 2 3 4 5
Trader Arjun Esha Chirag Divya Bela
Underlying INFY RELIANCE HDFC NIFTY BANKNIFTY
P&L +5 +1 +3 −2 +8

1.1 (c) Chirag. 1.2 +1. 1.3 Desk 4. 1.4 (c) HDFC — RELIANCE is at desk 2, so desk 3 is HDFC. 1.5 +3 — desk 1 (+5) and desk 4 (−2). 1.6 3. Without clue 9, all three branches survive: {Divya at 2 with NF/BNF at (2,3)}, {Divya at 4 with (3,4)}, and {Divya at 4 with (4,5)}.

The lesson: clue 9 looks like a throwaway negative constraint. It's the only thing making the set unique. In a grid set, always ask which constraint is doing the disambiguating work — that's usually the one you're meant to apply last, and forgetting it produces a set that "has no answer."

SET 2 — Round Robin

The entry point is the points total, not the individual teams. Total points = 7 + 4 + 4 + 1 = 16. Six matches are played. A decisive match puts 3 points into the pot, a drawn one puts 2. With d decisive matches: 3d + 2(6 − d) = 16 → d = 4, so 2 draws.

Now decompose each team's three results: - P = 7 = 3 + 3 + 1 → 2 wins, 1 draw - Q = 4 = 3 + 1 + 0 → 1 win, 1 draw, 1 loss - R = 4 = 3 + 1 + 0 → 1 win, 1 draw, 1 loss - S = 1 = 1 + 0 + 0 → 1 draw, 2 losses

Check: 4 wins, 4 losses, 4 draw-participations (= 2 draws). Consistent.

Every team drew exactly once, so the two draws pair up all four teams. Three pairings are possible: (P–S, Q–R), (P–Q, R–S), (P–R, Q–S). The clue "R did not draw with S" kills the middle one. The other two both work:

Scenario A Scenario B
Draws P–S, Q–R P–R, Q–S
P beat Q, beat R beat Q, beat S
Q beat S beat R
R beat S beat S
S — —

Verify A: P = 3+3+1 = 7 ✓, Q = 0+1+3 = 4 ✓, R = 0+1+3 = 4 ✓, S = 1+0+0 = 1 ✓. Verify B: P = 3+3+1 = 7 ✓, Q = 0+3+1 = 4 ✓, R = 1+0+3 = 4 ✓, S = 0+1+0 = 1 ✓.

2.1 2. 2.2 2 — S loses twice in both scenarios. 2.3 (c) S — R beats S in both scenarios. 2.4 (b) P beat Q — true in both. (a) holds only in A, (c) only in A, (d) only in B. 2.5 2.

The lesson: an under-determined set is not a broken set. CAT builds these deliberately and then asks "must be true" and "cannot be determined" questions against them. The correct move when you hit ambiguity is to enumerate every valid scenario and keep them all live, not to hunt for a missing clue. Candidates who assume they've misread something burn five minutes re-reading and then abandon a set they had already solved.

SET 3 — Desk P&L

The entry point is the FX row, which is the only line with exactly one unknown: 8 + f + 11 + 7 = 38 → FX Q2 = 12.

That unlocks the Q2 column: e + 9 + 12 + 15 = 47 → Equity Q2 = 11. Then the Equity row: 12 + 11 + 18 + x = 61 → Equity Q4 = 20.

Now the Commodity/Rates block. Commodity: c₁ + c₃ = 44 − 9 − 14 = 21. With the extra condition c₃ = 2c₁: c₁ = 7, c₃ = 14. Then Q1: 12 + 7 + 8 + r₁ = 40 → Rates Q1 = 13, and Q3: 18 + 14 + 11 + r₃ = 53 → Rates Q3 = 10.

Completed table:

Desk Q1 Q2 Q3 Q4 Year
Equity 12 11 18 20 61
Commodity 7 9 14 14 44
FX 8 12 11 7 38
Rates 13 15 10 10 48
Total 40 47 53 51 191

3.1 20. 3.2 (a) Equity. Equity 11 → 18 = +63.6%; Commodity 9 → 14 = +55.6%; FX and Rates both fell. 3.3 1. Only Q3 (FX 11 vs Rates 10). Q1: 8 < 13. Q2: 12 < 15. Q4: 7 < 10. 3.4 13. 3.5 (c) 57%. Equity 61 + Rates 48 = 109; 109/191 ≈ 57.1%. 3.6 (d) Rates Q1. FX Q2, Equity Q2 and Equity Q4 all follow from row and column totals alone. Rates Q1 depends on Commodity Q1, which without the extra condition is a free parameter — the Commodity and Rates rows have one degree of freedom between them.

The lesson: in a partial-table set, never start at the top-left. Scan for the row or column with exactly one unknown and start there, then cascade. The order in which you unlock cells is the entire difficulty of the set. Starting with Commodity Q1 (two unknowns, underdetermined without the rider) is how people conclude the set is unsolvable.

Post-mortem

Go back to the GO/MAYBE/NO ranking you wrote in the first 4 minutes.

Did your ranking match reality? That calibration — not your solve speed — is the thing this exercise is actually training. On the real exam you will rank four sets and solve three, and if your ranking is wrong you lose the section before you've solved a single question.

For any set you couldn't crack, answer only: what was the entry point I missed? In these three it was, respectively: fixing all five P&L values from the ordering clue before placing anyone; computing the total points to derive the number of draws; and finding the single row with one unknown. Each is a one-line observation that collapses the set. Collect these — they repeat across sets far more than the surface stories do.
