CAT, Decoded

The mechanics

120 minutes. 68 questions. 204 marks. Three sections, 40 minutes each, in fixed order.

VARC → DILR → QA. When the 40-minute clock on a section runs out, that section closes permanently. You cannot go back, you cannot bank unused time, you cannot start the next section early.

This single rule is the whole design of the exam. It means a bad VARC cannot be rescued by a strong QA within the exam — you just sit there watching a section you can't fix. It also means you cannot blow your time budget, because the budget is enforced.

Section composition (stable since 2021)

VARC — 24 questions, 72 marks - 4 Reading Comprehension passages, 4 questions each = 16 Q - 8 Verbal Ability questions, always some mix of: - Para-jumble (4–5 sentences, reorder them) — TITA, no negative marking - Para-summary (pick the best 1-sentence summary of a paragraph) — MCQ - Odd-one-out (5 sentences, one doesn't belong) — TITA, no negative marking

DILR — 22 questions, 66 marks - 4 sets, 5 or 6 questions each. Modern CAT DILR is closer to logic puzzles than to chart-reading. Expect: constraint grids, tournaments/scheduling, voting/elimination rounds, network flows, partially-filled tables with logical constraints.

QA — 22 questions, 66 marks Rough topic weights in recent years: - Arithmetic ~45% — ratio/proportion, percentages, profit-loss, time-speed-distance, time-work, mixtures, averages, SI/CI. This is the highest yield per minute in the entire exam. - Algebra ~25% — quadratics, inequalities, modulus, logarithms, functions, surds - Number system ~10% — remainders, factors, divisibility, base systems - Geometry & mensuration ~12% - Modern maths ~8% — P&C, probability, sequences & series, set theory

No calculator. There is an on-screen basic calculator (add/subtract/multiply/divide/sqrt). It is slow and you should almost never touch it.

Marking

Correct Wrong Unattempted
MCQ +3 −1 0
TITA +3 0 0

Typically 15–20 of the 68 are TITA. You should never leave a TITA question blank. A wild guess on a TITA is a free lottery ticket with no cost.

Notice how cheap DILR and QA percentiles are. 32 marks out of 66 in DILR — that's 11 correct out of 22 with zero wrong — is the 99th percentile. Most of the country cannot do this section at all. Same story in QA. VARC is the expensive one: you need 49/72, roughly 17 correct out of 24, to hit 99.

That asymmetry is the whole strategic picture.

What a 99th-percentile paper actually looks like for you

Not "solve everything." This:

Section Attempt Correct Raw Sectional %ile
VARC 17 12 31 ~86
DILR 17 14 39 ~99.5
QA 18 16 46 ~99.6
Total 52 / 68 42 116 ~99.2 overall

You leave 16 questions untouched and still land at 99.2. Internalise that. The people who try to attempt all 68 score 70.

The VARC line is the one to stare at. 12 correct out of 17 attempted is mediocre — and it still works, because DILR and QA carry the paper. Your job in VARC is not excellence. It's not blowing up: clear the sectional floor (~85th percentile, ~30 marks) and get out.

The sectional cutoff problem

IIMs shortlist on a composite score, and most of them impose minimum sectional percentiles. Roughly, for general category:

  • IIM Ahmedabad: ~80 in each section
  • IIM Bangalore: ~85 in each section
  • IIM Calcutta: ~80 in each section
  • Older IIMs (L, K, I): 80–85 in each

This is why VARC matters even though it's your weak section. A 99.8 overall with a 75 in VARC gets you rejected by A, B and C. Your VARC floor is 85th percentile, non-negotiable. That's about 30/72 — roughly 11–12 correct out of 16–17 attempted. Achievable in 35 hours. Getting VARC to 95 is not, so don't chase it.

The honest bit about calls

You should know the real scoreboard before you invest the hours. IIM composite scores weight academic diversity (non-engineering undergrad) and gender diversity. As a general-category male engineer, you are in the most crowded bucket there is. The practical bar for an IIM A/B/C call in that bucket is typically 99.5+, sometimes higher, even with strong academics and work experience.

Your 10th/12th/B.Tech scores and Optiver work-ex all feed the composite and help. But if the goal were "get into IIM A," the honest advice would be different. You said love of the game — so the goal here is a clean, well-played 99+, and everything in this plan is optimised for that.

Two notes anyway, since the information is cheap: - FMS Delhi, MDI, SPJIMR, IIT DoMS programmes and the newer IIMs have meaningfully lower bars and take CAT scores. - If you ever do want a call, the single highest-leverage lever is not more CAT prep — it's work experience months, which accrue by themselves.

The one mental model

Each section is a 40-minute trading session with a fixed inventory of 22–24 instruments, of wildly varying quality, and you can only fill a fraction of them.

  • Your edge is selection, not solving. Everyone at 99 can solve every question given unlimited time.
  • −1 is the spread. 90 seconds is the capital charge. The capital charge dominates completely.
  • The worst trade in CAT is a 4-minute question you get right. It cost you two you'd also have gotten right.
  • The EV threshold on a 4-option MCQ is p > 0.25 — eliminate one option and guessing is already +EV. But EV is not your binding constraint. Time is. Don't guess your way into a wrong-answer habit; guess only when the clock is dying or the question is TITA.
