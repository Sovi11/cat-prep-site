Resources

Keep this short. Material is not your constraint — hours are. Everything below is either free or cheap, and the free stuff is better than most of the paid stuff.

Past CAT papers — the single best resource, and it's free

The IIMs officially release the question papers and answer keys after each exam. CAT 2017 through CAT 2025 is roughly 27 slots of real questions — about 1,800 questions, all at exactly the right difficulty, written by the people who write your exam.

You will not exhaust this in 35 hours. Nothing you buy is better.

  • Official: iimcat.ac.in (previous years' papers section)
  • Mirrored with solutions at cracku.in, 2iim.com, iquanta.in — all have free archives

Use them like this:

  • Phase 0 diagnostic: CAT 2024 Slot 2, full paper, timed.
  • Phase 1 VARC: pull individual RC passages from 2019–2023 papers. Three per session.
  • Phase 1 DILR: pull individual sets from 2019–2023.
  • Phase 3: CAT 2025 papers — save these, they're the most recent signal.

Don't use 2015-and-earlier papers. The pattern changed materially in 2020–21 (100 min / 76 Q) and again in 2021 (120 min / 66–68 Q). Older DILR in particular is a different animal.

Mocks — buy one series

For Phase 2 you want mocks taken by a real cohort, because your raw score is meaningless without a percentile against ~20,000 other people. Self-scored past papers can't give you that, and calibration is most of what a mock is for.

Any of these is fine. They're roughly interchangeable; don't spend time choosing.

Provider Approx. cost Note
IMS SimCAT ₹2,500–4,000 Largest test-taking cohort, so the percentiles are the most meaningful
TIME AIMCAT ₹2,500–4,000 Large cohort, historically tough papers
Career Launcher ₹2,000–3,500 Good analytics dashboard
Cracku / iQuanta ₹1,000–2,500 Cheaper, smaller cohort, decent quality

You need 5 mocks, not 40. Buy the smallest package that gets you five, or the full series if the pricing is similar — but resist the urge to take more than the plan calls for. An unanalysed mock is worse than no mock, because it burns two hours and teaches you nothing.

One warning: mock papers from these providers are systematically harder than the real CAT, and their percentiles run conservative. Don't read a 95th percentile in a SimCAT as a forecast of 95 in CAT. Read the trend and the sectional balance, not the absolute number.

VARC reading — where CAT passages actually come from

CAT RC passages are drawn from long-form essay journalism and academic-adjacent writing. Read one essay every other day. This is background, not study time.

Source Why
Aeon (aeon.co) Philosophy, psychology, science, history. The closest single match to CAT's register.
Psyche (psyche.co) Aeon's shorter sibling. Good for 5-minute reps.
3 Quarks Daily Curated links across exactly CAT's topic spread.
The Browser Daily five-link curation. High density of CAT-shaped prose.
London Review of Books Dense literary/political essays. Hardest register — the philosophy-passage training ground.
The Caravan Indian long-form. Politics, history, sociology.
Nautilus, Quanta Science writing. Your easy-passage comfort zone — useful for pace, not for stretch.

Read the hard ones. Reading Quanta because it's comfortable trains nothing. The passages you lose marks on are the aesthetics and historiography ones, so read those.

Books — mostly skip them

You don't have the hours for a book. If you insist on exactly one:

  • "How to Prepare for Quantitative Aptitude for CAT" — Arun Sharma is the standard, but it's ~700 pages aimed at people starting from zero maths. You are not that person. At most, use it as a lookup table for a formula you've forgotten.
  • Skip all vocabulary books (Word Power Made Easy, etc.). CAT hasn't tested vocabulary directly since 2014.
  • Skip all grammar books. CAT has no grammar questions.

Tools

  • Timer: a visible physical countdown or a phone stopwatch face-up. Not a background timer you have to unlock to check.
  • Rough sheets: A4, unlined. The real exam gives you loose sheets and a pen — practise with the same.
  • Mock platform: take at least three of your five mocks on a computer, in a browser, with a mouse. The interface friction is real and you want it to be familiar.

What to ignore

  • YouTube "CAT strategy" videos. You have the strategy; it's in 03-playbook.md. Watching more of it is procrastination that feels like work.
  • Telegram/Reddit prep groups. Net negative: anxiety generators with a low information rate.
  • Anything promising "syllabus completion." CAT has no syllabus.
  • Sectional mocks from providers. Use real past-paper sections instead — same time cost, better questions.
